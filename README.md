# Diabetes Dataset Analysis 

## 1. Key Findings

**Finding 1: Distribution of outcomes**

The dataset contains 768 records. Of these, 500 (approximately 65.1%) have Outcome 0, while 268 (approximately 34.9%) have Outcome 1. Outcome 0 represents the larger group in this dataset.

**Finding 2: Glucose levels differ between outcome groups**

The average glucose level for Outcome 0 is approximately 109.98, compared with 141.26 for Outcome 1. This shows a noticeable difference in average glucose levels between the two groups.

**Finding 3: BMI and glucose relationship**

The scatter plot shows that glucose and BMI values vary across both outcome groups. The groups overlap, suggesting that BMI alone does not clearly separate all records into the two outcome categories.

**Finding 4: Age-group distribution**

The age-group chart shows that the number of records varies across age intervals. Comparing counts and outcome proportions by age group can help identify differences in the distribution of outcomes.

## 2. Suggestions

1. Review zero values in measurements such as glucose and BMI to determine whether they represent missing or invalid measurements before conducting further analysis.
2. Explore additional variables, including blood pressure, insulin and pregnancies, to understand how these characteristics differ between outcome groups.

## 3. Recommendations

1. Use the interactive dashboard to explore differences in glucose levels and outcome distributions across age and pregnancy groups.
2. In future analysis, compare the percentage of Outcome 1 within each age group, rather than only comparing raw counts, to account for differences in group sizes.

## 4. Overall Conclusion

The Tableau dashboard highlights differences in glucose levels and distributions of outcomes across age groups. The interactive filters allow users to explore patterns across different ages and pregnancy counts. The findings describe associations within the dataset and do not establish that any individual factor causes diabetes. Further analysis and appropriate clinical assessment would be needed before drawing medical conclusions.
